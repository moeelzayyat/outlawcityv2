## 1. Summary

**The engineering is the best of your three games.** Pure rules modules with 216 real unit tests, a six-remote network contract with validation on every handler, a write-through economy with a reward ledger and ambiguous-write reconciliation, server-side prices, idempotent purchases, and an NPC police car that drives a road graph at 0.1 ms per frame. `[Code]` Most Roblox projects never reach this.

**The game is a 30-minute prototype in the most competitive genre on Roblox.** Content today: two drivable cars, one store, one crime that pays, one police car, one 12-second jail, a 636×636-stud grid and a highway. A first session is exhausted in roughly 30–40 minutes, and there is nothing to spend money on after the $15,000 Muscle. `[Code: VehicleConfig, CityLayout]` It is unpublished and has never been played by anyone but the owner. The incumbents are Jailbreak (8B visits), Emergency Hamburg (1.4B visits, ~17–85K concurrent, 50-player servers with police/fire/medic/criminal roles) and Mad City, each with years of content. `[Market]`

**The last four days went to lighting, 50 stop lines, 4 street signs and a plan to enlarge the signs**, inside a 27-package audit loop (498 files) — while the three gameplay-integrity holes below sat open and no human but the owner has driven the car. `[Docs]`

**Verdicts**

| Lens | Verdict | One line |
|---|---|---|
| Player psychology | **REVISE** (confidence Medium) | No reason to choose it over Jailbreak; no signature; loop exhausted in one session. Fixable with one design decision. |
| Technical | **REVISE** | Excellent foundation; three HIGH integrity gaps and a home-grown persistence layer that works but is now yours to maintain. |
| Pending batch c4bebb5 (sign enlargement) | **APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS as a change; DO NOT DO IT under v2.0** | Signs are not what this game lacks. |
| Process | **REVISE** | Replace per-task auditor packages with the checkpoint protocol; replace "solo only" with Studio multi-client tests. |
| Portfolio | **Recommendation** | Of your three games, this is the weakest position: a crowded genre, no differentiator, the least content. Run it third, after Lighthouse Keepers reaches its public test and DEADZONE reaches A3.5 — or park it. The package below is written so it can run unattended if you choose to continue. |

**The one decision that changes the game (D-001):** give it a signature. **Crime Spree** — unbanked cash with a rising multiplier, banked only at a safehouse, lost when busted, police escalating with stars. It turns the existing wanted/escape/robbery code into a press-your-luck loop with a number as the goal ("Biggest Spree"), works solo-tested against NPC police, and fits 3–5-minute phone sessions.

## 3. Player-psychology audit

### Snapshot
- **Premise as a stranger hears it:** "GTA-style city where you rob a store, outrun one cop car, and buy a second car." Today that is the whole game.
- **Primary segment:** older kids / teens (10–15) who already play Jailbreak, Mad City or Emergency Hamburg; phone and PC. `[Inference]`
- **Need served:** power/outlaw fantasy, driving mastery, status via cars.
- **Incumbents:** Jailbreak (8B visits; ~4–7K CCU now, 530K peak in 2020), Emergency Hamburg (1.4B visits; ~17K CCU, 85K peak Mar 2026), Mad City. `[Market]`
- **Team:** solo owner + Claude; unpublished; $3,500 budget stated in the roadmap.

### Walkthrough (13-year-old, phone)
- **Icon & title:** none exist.
- **0–30 s:** plaza, tip card, two markers, one tap to a Coupe. Good. `[Docs]`
- **30 s–2 min:** drive 150 studs, hold E for 15 s doing nothing, one cruiser spawns. The Combat button does nothing.
- **2–5 min:** escape by breaking line of sight for ~20 s. Scripted getaways paid 5/5 with the tuned police — probably too easy — and 0/2 in the next round; no human data. `[Docs 8i, 8r]` +$2,000. Store closed for 3 minutes.
- **5–30 min:** repeat the same store 8 times to afford the Muscle. Then nothing.
- **Day 2:** no reason exists.

### Section findings
1. **Audience fit (2)** — big, proven segment; the fantasy is right; demand is not the problem.
2. **Click appeal (1)** — nothing to click; the name promises a city it doesn't have.
3. **First session (2)** — onboarding work (P1–P8) is good; the 15-second idle holdout and the inert Combat button are the weak moments.
4. **Core loop (1)** — one crime, one store, one cop, one escape. No decisions after the first run. `[Practitioner: one mechanic → many decisions]`
5. **Progression & return (1)** — one purchase, then a wall; reputation is session-only and unused. `[Code: PROGRESS 8]`
6. **Social (1)** — no PvP damage, no co-op, no shared goal; the second player breaks the police (F2).
7. **Differentiation (0)** — no element is only yours; the incumbents have everything here plus a thousand more things. `[Practitioner: proven reason + only-yours]`
8. **Monetization (2)** — nothing enabled; policy (cosmetics/convenience, no power) is sound.
9. **Execution risk (2)** — foundation excellent; content velocity near zero; the process is the bottleneck.
10. **Validation (1)** — unpublished; one owner session logged; no real players ever.

### Why players would quit (ranked)
1. Never find it (no listing, no icon).
2. Minute 4, the first escape: too easy (5/5) or impossible (0/2) — balance untested by humans.
3. Minute 20: the same store for the eighth time.
4. Minute 35: the Muscle is bought; nothing left.
5. Any second player: free escapes, griefed cars, a Combat button that lies.

### Playtest plan (S3)
- Build: integrity fixes + Crime Spree + 3 crime types + escalating police + audio + HUD scale. 8-player servers.
- Riskiest assumptions: a phone player completes and banks a spree in ≤ 5 minutes; ≥ 50% start a second spree; bust rate 30–60% (not 0, not 100).
- Measure: first-play bounce, D1, qualified sessions, co-play days `[Official]`; spree completion, bank rate, bust rate, "one more" rate.

## 4. Process audit
Twenty-seven audit packages (498 files) with hashes, rollback demonstrations and before/after screenshots, for a game with two cars. The evidence discipline is admirable; the granularity is the problem — four days on street signs. The checkpoint protocol keeps tests, play test, numbers, restore point and mirror, and drops the per-task relay. The "solo Studio tests only" policy is also unnecessary: Studio's Test tab runs a local server with 2–8 clients, which covers dispatch, car ownership, spawn pads and most of `MULTIPLAYER_TESTS.md` without real players.
